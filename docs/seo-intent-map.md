# SEO Intent Map — Check My Next Home

This map is the plan for what the site targets in search. Every public page on the site exists to answer one search intent — the question a home mover is actually asking — and each intent has exactly one owning page. Rule: if two pages target the same intent, merge them. One intent, one best page — no cannibalisation.

No public page is built without a row on this map. If a new page is ever proposed, its intent and row are added here first.

All URLs below are paths on the canonical host, checkmynexthome.co.uk. The site reports facts and counts only — no page on this map promises a score, a rating of an area, or a verdict on whether somewhere is a good or bad place to live.

M6.10 (decision D38) locked the wording layer behind this map: every owning page's title and meta description live as constants in `lib/seo.ts` (PAGE_SEO), written under the locked rules — no figures in any title or meta, ever; meta descriptions descriptive with the as-published frame; a title may carry the searcher's question verbatim, never our answer to it; titles and descriptions are per-page, set by the page, never inherited from the layout. The same step corrected rows 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and 13 to what the built brain actually serves, made row 9 guide-only, and added rows 17–20. There are NO town or area pages in Version 1 (D38): every area fact the product holds is a postcode-point fact or a small-area statistic it will not turn into an area verdict, so no page aggregates to town level; a town page could only ever enter through this map, as a new row, first.

## Never targeted

These intents get no page, no title and no meta — deliberately. A future page proposal meets this list here first.

1. **The Check Together gift** — a surprise inside the paid delivery email only (D21). It is never mentioned in any public wording: no page, title, meta, heading or structured data refers to it, in any words.
2. **Benefits and deprivation verdicts** — Census, IMD and benefits data are report depth only (D26) and never appear on the landing page (D12). Queries like 'how deprived is [area]' or 'benefits in [area]' are not given a page.
3. **Company ownership** — filed behind a default-off flag (D18). While the flag is off, no row and no copy may promise or surface it.
4. **Building-safety register lookups** — no published list exists and register absence can never support a line (D17). There is no 'is my building on the register' page; the tribunal facts and the register hand-off live inside the check, not as a search promise.
5. **A home's own council tax band** — never stated (D16). Row 8 answers the band query with the council's scale instead.
6. **School catchment verdicts** — 'will my child get in', catchment positions: no catchment data is held (row 6).
7. **'Is [area] safe' as our wording** — row 4 targets the phrase; the page never adopts it (D24).
